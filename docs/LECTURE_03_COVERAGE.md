## One L03 Project Assignment — content brief, not a new form

**Marketing Decision Journey phase:** refine the already discussed project idea into two plausible, meaningfully different customer segments, and state what evidence would test the difference.

- **Do:** draft two distinct potential segments. For each, note relevant B2C or B2B segmentation criteria and the actual unmet need/benefit expected. Do not present “B2C” alone as a customer segment.
- **Evidence:** label assumptions explicitly; identify one or more research questions / primary or credible secondary sources to test the proposed difference. Do not assert any survey/interview findings that have not been collected.
- **Decision:** record which Marketing decision (e.g. packaging, channel, offer, communication or buyer reach) would actually differ if evidence supports the grouping. Defer final Targeting/Positioning until their criteria are academically covered and tested.
- **Output:** **one compact two-segment comparison** inside the same cumulative Marketing Plan, not a second file/submission.
- **Acceptance:** explain why the groups are meaningfully different, what could disprove each assumption, and the next data collection step. Review the five evaluation criteria after reinforcement.
- **Progress Review:** a short oral “what was tested / what evidence or obstacle emerged / next justified decision” rather than a compulsory weekly upload.

## Scientific and privacy safeguards

- Market/factory counts inferred from Maps or AI search are exploratory; not an exhaustive census.
- Anecdotal local sales, crop yields, international deals or chain-store practices are not independently established facts.
- “Repeat Purchase” on its own does not show “Loyalty”; test alternative explanations.
- Small B2B segments are not automatically unsuitable: **Substantial** depends on attainable value and operating economics, not a universal head-count cutoff.
- Dietary/medical suitability claims (diabetes, gluten-free, lactose-free, “healthy”) require appropriate substantiation and safety review. Do not imply suitability from a proposed name or student claim.
- A Perceptual Map featuring real car brands cannot be presented as a customer perception finding without a real data source; use clearly hypothetical labels and values if illustrative.
- Do not publish participant names, unreleased team business ideas, intellectual-property-sensitive product formulations or private evidence. A participant explicitly raised an IP concern during group presentations; do not infer consent.
- Do not copy educator-only comments, facilitator timings, the full Trainer PDF or raw transcripts into student HTML or GitHub.
